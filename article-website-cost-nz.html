How much does a small business website cost in NZ?

By the Tova Digital team · 18 November 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

At Tova, a starter site is $2,500, a middle site $3,500 to $5,000 and a premium site $6,000 to $8,000, all excluding GST. Price rises with the number of pages, how custom the design is, the features you need and who writes the content. Hosting and care are ongoing extras.

"How much for a website?" is one of the most common questions we hear, and one of the hardest to find a straight answer to online. Many web studios stay vague, which makes it tricky to know whether a quote is fair.

So we'll be plain about it. We're Tova Digital, a small web design studio in Ōtautahi Christchurch, and we publish our starting prices. This article explains what moves the price, what each tier usually covers, how GST works and what you'll pay after launch. At the end there's a checklist for comparing quotes like for like.

What drives the cost of a small business website?

The price comes down to how much thinking, design and building your website needs. A website is not a product off a shelf, so the same two words, "a website", can describe very different amounts of work.

These are the things that move the price most:

  • Size. A handful of pages is quicker to design and build than a site with dozens.
  • Custom design. A layout shaped around your brand and customers takes more time than one built from a ready-made starting point.
  • Features. Online bookings, a shop, member logins, or connections to other software each add work.
  • Words and photos. Someone has to write the text and find or take the pictures. If that's you, the price is lower. If it's us, it's higher.
  • Findability. Building the site so search engines understand it takes care, and some projects include more of it than others.
  • Who does the work. A studio where design and engineering sit side by side prices differently to a large agency with layers of staff.

If a quote doesn't tell you which of these it includes, ask. That's usually where the gaps are hiding.

What does a starter website usually include?

A starter website is a simple, clear online home for your business, and ours is $2,500 excluding GST: a simple static site, like an online business card. It's the right size for many sole traders and small local businesses.

In general, a starter site covers the essentials: a small number of pages, a clean design that works on phones, and a way for customers to contact you. Its job is to say who you are, what you do and how to get in touch, and to do that well.

What it usually doesn't cover is anything heavily custom, such as a shop, bookings or lots of pages. Exactly what's included varies from studio to studio, which is why a written quote matters.

What do middle and premium websites add?

A middle site, $3,500 to $5,000 excluding GST, generally means more pages, more design care and more features. A premium site, $6,000 to $8,000 excluding GST, is a full build with its own content management system (CMS), for businesses with bigger goals or more complex needs.

Here is the usual pattern across the market, rather than a promise of what any one quote contains:

  • Starter: a simple static site, like an online business card: a tidy design and a clear way to get in touch. Good for getting found and looking professional.
  • Middle: a larger site with more tailored design, more content, and room for features such as enquiry forms that do more or links into other tools.
  • Premium: a full build with its own content management system, so the site can grow with you, often with a shop, bookings or integrations, deeper design and brand work, and more planning up front.

We quote every project individually, so you know the full cost before we begin. The tiers are a guide to where you're likely to land, not a fixed menu. You can read more about the kind of work behind them on our services section.

Does the price include GST?

Not always, so check every time. Our prices exclude GST, and GST of 15% is added when we invoice. For example, a $2,500 website becomes $2,875 once GST is added.

If your business is GST-registered, you can usually claim that GST back, which means the real cost to you is the figure before GST. Your accountant can tell you how it applies to your situation.

The reason this matters is simple: a price quoted including GST can look higher than one quoted excluding it, even when the work is identical. Always compare like with like.

What are the ongoing costs of a website?

A website has running costs after launch, just as a shopfront has rent and maintenance. They're usually smaller than the build, but they're worth knowing about before you sign.

The common ones are:

  • Domain name: your web address, which is renewed regularly.
  • Hosting: the service that keeps your site online and fast.
  • Updates and security: keeping the software current and protected.
  • Changes and support: help when you want a page edited or something goes wrong.
  • Growth work: ongoing search and marketing effort, if you want it.

Some studios fold these into the price and others charge separately. Neither is wrong, but you should know which it is. After launch we stay, to support, improve and help you grow, and we'd rather you hear about those costs at the start than discover them later.

How do you compare website quotes like for like?

Compare quotes line by line, not by the bottom figure. Two quotes with very different totals are often pricing different things, and the cheaper one may simply leave out what the dearer one includes.

Hold each quote up against this checklist:

  • Is the price shown excluding or including GST?
  • Which pages and features are listed, and how many?
  • Is the design custom, and how many rounds of feedback do you get?
  • Who writes the words and who sources the photos?
  • Are hosting, your domain name, security and updates included, or extra, and what do they cost each year?
  • Will you be shown how to edit the site yourself?
  • Will the domain and logins be in your name, so you own what you've paid for?
  • What happens after launch, and who do you contact?
  • What counts as extra, and will it be priced before any work starts?

If a quote can't answer one of these, that's a fair question to send back. We cover more on choosing well in our guide on how to choose a web designer in Christchurch.

So what should you do?

Start with what you need the website to do, then match it to a tier rather than the other way round. A clear idea of your goal saves money, because you pay for what helps and skip what doesn't.

  • Write a few sentences on what the site should do for your business.
  • Decide whether you'll supply the words and photos or want help.
  • Ask two or three studios for written quotes, excluding GST, covering the same things.
  • Use the checklist above to compare them line by line.
  • Ask about ongoing costs before you say yes.

Frequently asked questions

Is a $2,500 website enough for a small business?

For many small businesses, yes. A simple starter site that says who you are, what you do and how to get in touch can do a lot of work. If you need online bookings, a shop or lots of pages, a middle or premium site is usually a better fit.

Do website prices in NZ include GST?

Not always, so check. Many studios, including us, quote excluding GST, and GST of 15% is added to the invoice. If you are GST-registered you can usually claim it back, and your accountant can confirm how it applies to you.

Why do two website quotes for the same business look so different?

They are often pricing different things. One may include copywriting, photography, training and ongoing care while the other leaves them out. Lay the quotes side by side and compare them line by line before you compare the totals.

What do I pay for after my website is built?

Usually your domain name, hosting, and some form of updates, security and support. Some of these may be included in the quote and some may be separate. Ask for each one to be listed in writing so there are no surprises later.
