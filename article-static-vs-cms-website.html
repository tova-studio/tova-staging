What is the difference between a static site and a CMS site?

By the Tova Digital team · 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

A static site is a fixed set of pages built by a developer; to change the words, you ask them. A CMS site has an editing area, so you can change text, images and pages yourself. Static is simpler, faster and needs less upkeep. A CMS suits businesses that update often.

If you've been getting quotes for a website, you may have noticed two kinds of proposal. One is a simple site, and the other comes with something called a CMS. The price usually differs, and the explanation often doesn't help much.

This article is written by Tova Digital, a web design studio in Ōtautahi Christchurch with design and engineering under one roof. We'll explain both types in plain words, compare them on editing, speed, security and upkeep, and finish with a quick guide to which suits which kind of business.

What is a static website?

A static website is a set of finished pages that sit on a server and are shown to visitors exactly as they were built. Every visitor sees the same thing, and nothing is assembled behind the scenes.

Think of it like a printed brochure, but on screen. The words, photos and layout are all fixed in place. If you want to change your opening hours or add a new service, someone with the right skills updates the files and puts the site live again.

That doesn't mean a static site is dull or basic. It can have a beautiful custom design, forms, maps and animations. "Static" describes how the pages are delivered, not how good they look.

What is a CMS website, and is it the same as WordPress?

A CMS, or content management system, is a private editing area behind your website where you log in and change things yourself. You type into screens a bit like a document or an email, press save, and the site updates.

WordPress is the best-known example, so many people use the two words as if they mean the same thing. They don't. WordPress is one CMS among many, and a CMS can also be built around one business, with only the screens that business needs. If you're searching for a WordPress alternative, that's worth knowing: the real choice is often between a ready-made system and one shaped to fit you.

Behind the scenes, a CMS site usually stores your content in a database and builds pages from it. That's what makes editing possible, and it's also where the extra upkeep comes from, as you'll see below.

Which is easier to edit?

A CMS is easier to edit, because it was designed for exactly that. You can fix a typo, add a blog post or swap a photo in minutes without waiting for anyone.

With a static site, every change goes through your web team. That's fine if you only change things once or twice a year. It gets frustrating if you'd like to post news every week or update prices regularly.

The honest question is how often you really will edit. Plenty of owners imagine updating their site often, then find it rarely changes. Others know they'll be in there every week. Be realistic about which one you are.

Which is faster?

Static sites tend to be faster, because there's less work to do before a page appears. The pages are already finished and ready to send.

A CMS site often builds each page on request, pulling content from the database first. A well-built CMS site can still be quick, so this isn't a reason to rule one out. But heavy themes, lots of add-ons and cheap hosting can slow things down, and speed affects how visitors feel and how Google sees your site.

Which is more secure?

A static site has less that can go wrong, so it generally has less to attack. There's no login screen and no database for someone to try to break into.

A CMS has more moving parts. The editing area, the database and any add-ons all need to be kept up to date, because out-of-date software is a common way sites get compromised. That's very manageable with proper care, but it is ongoing work, not a one-off job.

Neither type is magically safe. What matters is who is looking after it, and whether they're doing it regularly.

What does upkeep look like for each?

A static site needs very little upkeep, while a CMS site needs steady, regular care. This is the difference people most often overlook when comparing quotes.

Here's what that care usually involves:

  • Static site: hosting, an occasional check that everything still works, and a call to your web team when you want a change.
  • CMS site: hosting, regular updates to the system and its add-ons, backups, security checks, and some time from you to keep the content fresh.

On the price side, in our own pricing a simple static site starts at NZ$2,500 and a full build with its own content management system is NZ$6,000 to NZ$8,000, both excluding GST. Our article on what a small business website costs in NZ explains what moves the price up or down.

Which one fits your business? A quick guide

The right choice depends mostly on how often your content changes and who will be changing it. Use these business types as a rough guide, not a rule.

  • Sole trader or tradie who needs a solid online presence: static is usually plenty. A few pages, clear services, a phone number and a contact form.
  • Professional service, such as an accountant, lawyer or consultant: static often works well, because the information changes slowly. Choose a CMS if you plan to publish articles regularly.
  • Cafe, restaurant or retailer with changing menus, specials or stock: a CMS makes sense, so you can update things yourself the same day.
  • Business that publishes news, articles or case studies often: a CMS is the better fit, since adding posts should be easy.
  • Team with several people who each need to edit their own pages: a CMS, ideally with different access for different people.
  • Business that's just starting out and still working out its offer: start static and keep costs low. You can grow into a CMS later.

If you're stuck between two, ask yourself one thing. In the next twelve months, how many times do you expect to change something on the site? If the answer is a handful, static. If it's dozens, a CMS.

So what should you do next?

Decide how much editing you'll really do, then choose the simplest type that covers it. Paying for a CMS you never log into is wasted money, and a static site you constantly need changed is a daily frustration.

A few steps help:

  1. List the parts of your site that will change, and how often.
  2. Decide who will make those changes: you, a staff member or your web team.
  3. Ask any designer you speak to who handles updates and security afterwards, and what that costs.
  4. Ask whether you can start simple and add a CMS later if you outgrow it.

Frequently asked questions

Is WordPress a CMS?

Yes. WordPress is the best-known content management system, but it is only one of many. A CMS can also be built to suit a single business, with only the editing screens that business needs.

Can I start with a static site and add a CMS later?

Usually, yes. If the original site is built cleanly, a CMS can be added when your needs grow. It is worth mentioning your plans at the start so the design leaves room for them.

Is a static website bad for SEO?

No. Search engines read static pages very well, and simple, quick pages can be a strength. What matters most is clear, useful content and a sensible structure, not which type of site you have.

Is a CMS site less secure than a static site?

A CMS has more moving parts, so it has more to keep up to date and protect. That is manageable with proper care, but it is ongoing work. A static site has less to attack because there is no login area or database.
