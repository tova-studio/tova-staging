How do I show up in Google in Christchurch?

By the Tova Digital team · 19 November 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

Start with a complete, accurate Google Business Profile, then make sure your business name, address and phone number match everywhere online. Ask happy customers for reviews, give each service and area a clear page on your website, and check the basics like mobile use and speed. Most of this you can do yourself in a month.

If you run a business in Christchurch, you have probably typed your own business name into Google and wondered why a competitor appeared first. It's a common worry, and it has a more hopeful answer than most people expect.

Getting found locally is mostly about a handful of sensible habits, done in the right order. This article is written by Tova Digital, a small web design studio in Ōtautahi Christchurch, and it's designed so you can work through it yourself without an agency.

What does "showing up on Google" actually mean?

It means appearing when a nearby customer searches for what you do, in the places they actually look. For local searches, that is usually two places: the map section with a short list of businesses, and the regular results below it.

Think of someone searching "plumber Christchurch" or "café near me" on their phone. Google tries to show businesses that are close, relevant and well regarded. Those three ideas, closeness, relevance and reputation, sit behind everything in this article.

Nobody can buy or guarantee a top spot. What you can do is give Google clear, honest, consistent information about your business, and give customers good reasons to trust it.

Why start with your Google Business Profile?

Your Google Business Profile is a free listing that controls how your business appears on Google Search and Google Maps, so it's the best first step for most local businesses. It's often the first thing a customer sees, before they ever reach your website.

Search for your business name. If a panel appears on the right or at the top, you may already have a profile, perhaps one that Google created or someone set up years ago. Claim it and verify that it's yours. Google will walk you through how to prove that.

If you don't have one, create it. If you work from home or travel to customers, you can usually hide your street address and list the areas you serve instead.

How do you fill in your profile properly?

Fill in every section honestly and keep it up to date. An incomplete profile gives customers and Google less to go on, and an out-of-date one can send people to a closed door.

Here is what to check:

  • Your business name, exactly as it appears on your signage and website. Don't add keywords to it.
  • The right main category, plus any extra categories that truly apply.
  • Opening hours, including public holidays such as Canterbury Show Day.
  • Your phone number, website address and service areas.
  • A short description in plain words of what you do and who you help.
  • Your services or products, listed clearly.
  • Real photos of your premises, your team and your work, not stock images.

Once a month, take a few minutes to look at it again. A fresh photo or a quick update shows the business is active.

Why do your name, address and phone number need to match?

Because Google cross-checks your details across the internet, and consistent details make it more confident that you are one real, reliable business. If your name or address is written three different ways, it has to guess.

Pick one version of your business name, address and phone number, and use it everywhere. Check these places first:

  • Your website, especially the footer and contact page.
  • Your Google Business Profile.
  • Your Facebook and Instagram pages and any LinkedIn page.
  • Business directories and industry listings you've signed up to over the years.
  • Your email signature and invoices.

Small differences matter less than big ones, so don't lose sleep over "Street" versus "St". But an old address, a disconnected phone number or a former trading name is worth fixing. Moved premises since the earthquakes or a rebrand along the way? Old listings are exactly where outdated details tend to linger.

How do reviews help, and how do you get them?

Reviews help in two ways: they persuade the person reading, and they signal to Google that real customers value you. The simplest way to get them is to ask, at the moment a customer is happiest.

Google gives you a direct link to your review page. Send it by text or email after a job is done, or put it on a card at the counter. Keep the request short and friendly, and make it easy to say yes.

A few ground rules keep you on the right side of things:

  • Never pay for reviews or write your own. It's against Google's rules and customers can usually tell.
  • Ask everyone, not just the people you expect to praise you.
  • Reply to every review, kindly and briefly, including the awkward ones.

A calm, professional reply to a poor review often earns more trust than a spotless record. It shows how you treat people when things go wrong.

Does your website need local pages?

Yes, if you serve different services or areas, because each page gives Google and your customers a clear answer to a specific question. A single page that tries to cover everything rarely does any of it well.

Give each main service its own page, written in the words your customers use. Explain what it involves, who it's for and how to get in touch. If you work in several parts of Canterbury, a page for each area you genuinely serve can help, as long as it has something real to say about that area.

Avoid copying the same text and swapping in a suburb name. It reads badly to people and does little for search. Mention Christchurch naturally, in your headings and contact details, and let the rest of the page be useful.

Which website basics matter most?

The basics are a site that works well on a phone, loads quickly, is easy to read and makes it obvious how to contact you. Fancy extras count for far less than these.

Check your site on your own phone, with mobile data, as if you were a stranger. Can you find your phone number straight away? Do pages load without a long wait? Is the text readable without zooming?

Then look at the quiet details:

  • Each page has its own clear title and short description that say what the page is about.
  • Your address and phone number are written as text, not only inside an image.
  • The site uses a secure address, starting with https.
  • Your pages link to each other sensibly, so people and Google can find their way around.

What should you do in your first month?

Work through the steps in order, because each one builds on the last. You don't need to finish everything in a week, and steady progress beats a rush.

  1. Week one: claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Verify it, choose your categories, add your hours, description, services and some real photos.
  2. Week one: decide on your official name, address and phone number. Write them down in one place so you can copy them exactly.
  3. Week two: fix your details elsewhere. Update your website, social pages and any directories so they match your profile.
  4. Week three: start asking for reviews. Save your review link, then send it to recent happy customers. Reply to any reviews you already have.
  5. Week three: check your website on your phone. Fix anything slow, unclear or hard to read, and make your contact details easy to find.
  6. Week four: improve or add your service pages. Start with your most important service and write it for a customer, not for Google.
  7. Week four: set a reminder. Put a monthly slot in your diary to update your profile, ask for reviews and look at how people find you.

After that, it's maintenance and patience. Results build over time, so keep an eye on enquiries from your profile and website rather than checking your ranking every day.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to show up on Google in Christchurch?

It varies, and nobody can promise an exact date. A verified Google Business Profile can appear fairly soon, while website changes and new reviews tend to build up gradually. Keep going steadily and check your progress every month or two.

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes, creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. You do not need to pay anyone to claim it, although you will need to verify that the business is yours. Be wary of anyone who charges to do this and promises top ranking in return.

Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

A profile on its own can get you found, but a website gives customers more to trust and gives Google more to understand. It is the place to explain your services, show your work and answer questions. Most businesses do best with both working together.

Can I do local SEO myself without an agency?

Yes, the first steps are well within reach of a busy owner: a complete profile, consistent details, a steady flow of reviews and a clear website. Help becomes useful when the website needs rebuilding or you want ongoing measurement. Until then, the checklist in this article is a good start.
