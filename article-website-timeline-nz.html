How long does it take to build a website in NZ?

By the Tova Digital team · 19 November 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

It depends on the size of the site, but the build itself usually isn't the slow part. Most of the time goes on four steps: listen, shape, build and grow. Projects usually run late because content and feedback arrive late, so having your words, photos and decisions ready is the quickest way to launch sooner.

Nearly everyone asks this before they enquire, and it's a fair question. You might have a busy season coming, or a business that's simply waiting for its website to catch up. Yet the answers out there are usually vague, or a number that has nothing to do with your project.

This article is written by Tova Digital, a web design studio in Ōtautahi Christchurch with design and engineering under one roof. We won't promise you a number of weeks, because that would only be a guess. What we can do is show you where the time goes, and what you can do to shorten it.

How long does it take to build a website in NZ?

It depends mainly on how big the site is and how quickly the words, photos and decisions come together. A simple site of a few pages is a very different job from a custom design with its own content management system, which is the tool that lets you edit pages yourself.

Here's the part most people don't expect. The building itself is usually not the longest stretch. Time tends to disappear in the waiting: for a paragraph that hasn't been written, a photo that hasn't been taken, or a reply that hasn't been sent.

That's good news, because waiting is the part you have the most say over.

What are the four steps of a website project?

Our projects follow four steps: listen, shape, build and grow. Each has a clear job, and each depends on the one before it.

  1. Listen. A real conversation about your business, your people and where you want to go.
  2. Shape. Strategy and design you can see, click through and refine before we build.
  3. Build. Clean, fast, accessible code, tested carefully and handed over with training.
  4. Grow. After launch, we measure, improve and report back in plain language, every month.

Notice that the first steps happen before anyone writes a line of code. That's on purpose. Changing a design on screen is far easier than changing a finished build.

What happens in the listen and shape steps?

Listen is a conversation, and shape is where you see the result before it's built. Together they set the direction for everything that follows.

In listen, we ask about your business, your customers and what you want the website to do. This is where you're the expert, and we're there to ask good questions. The clearer you are about who the site is for and what a visitor should do next, the smoother everything after it goes.

In shape, we turn that conversation into strategy and design you can click through. You react, we refine, and we go around again until it feels right. This is the step where your feedback matters most, and where projects most often stall. A design left sitting in an inbox quietly pauses the whole project with it.

What happens in the build step?

In build, the approved design becomes a real, working website. Our engineer writes clean, fast, accessible code, and we test it carefully before it goes anywhere near the public.

This is also when your content goes in: the words, photos and details that make the site yours. If it's ready, it drops in smoothly. If it isn't, the build has to wait, or the site goes live with gaps that someone has to come back and fill.

Once everything has been tested, we hand the site over with training, so you know how to look after it. Then it goes live.

What happens after launch, in the grow step?

Launch isn't the end for us. Grow is where we measure how the site is doing, improve it and report back in plain language, every month.

That's useful for your timeline, too. A website doesn't have to be perfect on day one, because it can keep improving. Holding back a launch until every last page is polished is a common reason sites take longer than they need to.

What actually delays a website project?

Late content and slow feedback are the most common causes, and both are about people rather than technology. A few others turn up often as well.

  • Content that arrives late. Words and photos are often left until last, but the site can't be finished without them.
  • Feedback that takes a while. Each pause waits for your reply before the next step can begin.
  • Too many opinions. When several people comment separately, the feedback can pull in different directions.
  • Decisions reopened. Changing direction after a design is approved sends the project back a step.
  • Missing logins. Nobody can find the details for the domain name or hosting.

None of this is anyone's fault. Everyone running a business is busy, and a website is rarely the most urgent thing on the list. But once you know where delays come from, you can plan around them.

What can you prepare so you launch sooner?

Getting a few things ready before you start is the quickest way to shorten a project. Nothing needs to be perfect. A rough draft is far better than nothing.

  • Know your goal. One sentence on what a visitor should do: call, book, request a quote or buy.
  • Draft your words. Who you are, what you offer, who it's for and how to contact you. Rough is fine.
  • Gather your photos and logo. Find the original files, and check you have permission to use them.
  • Collect proof. Customer reviews or testimonials, with their okay to use them.
  • Choose one decision-maker. One person who gives the final yes, even if others share their thoughts first.
  • Find your logins. Your domain name, current hosting and business email details.
  • Block out time for feedback. Put it in your diary now, so replies don't wait behind a busy week.
  • Share your date, and why. If you want to be live before a busy season or the summer break, tell us at the start so we can plan around it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a website be built quickly if I'm in a hurry?

Sometimes, if your content and decisions are ready. Tell us your date at the start and we'll say honestly what is realistic. A rushed launch with gaps usually costs more time later than it saves.

Why does a bigger website take longer?

More pages mean more words, photos and decisions, and each one has to be written, designed, built and checked. A site with its own content management system, which lets you edit pages yourself, also has more to build and test. Size and complexity make a big difference to the timeline.

Do I need all my content ready before we start?

No. A rough draft is enough to begin, and the design can be shaped around it. What slows a project down is when important pieces arrive late or keep changing.

Is the website finished once it launches?

Not for us. Our fourth step, grow, is about measuring how the site performs and improving it. We report back in plain language, every month.

How do I find out how long my website would take?

Tell us about your business, the pages you think you need and when you'd like to be live. We'll reply within one working day and talk it through. Once we know what's involved, we can give you a clear picture.
